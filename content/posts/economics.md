## Basic Knowledge

## Security

Securities are tradable financial assets that represent ownership, debt, or other rights. They include stocks, bonds, mutual funds, derivatives, and more.

## Index

A market index is like a financial thermometer for a specific part of the market. Here’s what it does:

- Measures performance: It tracks the average price movement of a group of stocks, bonds, or other assets (e.g., "How did big U.S. companies do today?").
- Acts as a benchmark: Investors use it to compare their own portfolio’s performance (e.g., "Did my investments beat the S&P 500 this year?").
- Reflects trends: It shows whether a sector, country, or asset class is rising or falling (e.g., "Tech stocks are up 10% this month").

Examples:

- S&P 500: Tracks 500 large U.S. companies.
- Dow Jones Industrial Average: Tracks 30 "blue-chip" U.S. stocks.
- Nasdaq Composite: Focuses on tech companies.

> [!QUESTION] Can you buy a mark index?
> No, you can’t buy an index directly—it’s just a measurement tool, like a weather report. But you can invest in funds that copy the index!

## Funds

### Index Fund

An index fund is a type of investment fund (like a mutual fund or ETF) that aims to **copy the performance of a specific market index**, such as the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100.

Instead of trying to pick individual stocks (which is risky and time-consuming), the fund buys all (or most) of the stocks/bonds in that index. If the index goes up 5%, the fund should roughly go up 5% too.

## Stocks

Dividends are payments made by a company to its shareholders, usually from profits. They're like a reward for owning the company's stock.

### Stocks vs Bonds

The main differences are:

Ownership vs. Debt

- Stocks (股份): Represent partial ownership in a company. Shareholders may receive dividends and have voting rights.
- Bonds (债券): Represent loans to a company or government. Bondholders receive fixed interest payments and repayment of principal.

Risk & Return

- Stocks: Higher risk (value fluctuates with the company’s performance) but potentially higher returns.
- Bonds: Lower risk (fixed interest) but generally lower returns.

Income Type

- Stocks: Dividends (not guaranteed).
- Bonds: Regular interest (guaranteed if the issuer is solvent).

> [!TIP]
> Bondholders are paid before shareholders if a company goes bankrupt.

## Portfolio

A portfolio is simply your collection of investments — like a financial "toolbox" where you keep all the money-growing tools you own.

It can include any (or all!) of these:

- Stocks: Shares of companies (e.g., Apple, Tesla).
- Bonds: Loans to governments or corporations (safer, lower returns).
- Cash: Savings accounts, CDs, or physical cash.
- Real estate: Property you own or REITs (real estate investment trusts).
- Funds: Index funds, mutual funds, ETFs (e.g., S&P 500 fund).
- Alternative investments: Gold, crypto, art, etc
